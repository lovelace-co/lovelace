## Symptom (user reports)

When two or more agents work a project at once (parallel subagents inside one session, or two sessions side by side), the blue live ring flickers, jumps to the wrong ticket card or resets its elapsed timer, and sometimes a ticket shows in the wrong board column.

## Outcome (2026-07-27)

All five suspected mechanisms were confirmed and fixed. Mechanisms 2, 3 and 4 were reproduced with multi-process stress harnesses against the demo fixture; mechanism 1 was demonstrated deterministically with the same core calls the hooks make; mechanism 5 is deterministic from the code path. ADR-0016 records the design. A fresh-context adversarial review of the first fix round surfaced and led to fixes for: a non-atomic lock steal path (two waiters could both enter the critical section), a lock release that could throw after a steal or remove another holder's lock, `setColumnOrder` and the host's document writes left outside the new protections, file mode lost on atomic rewrite, and a regression where the deferred-event replay raised the external-change toast after every app mutation.

## Mechanisms, as confirmed

1. **Ring hops and singleton leak.** Confirmed both halves. A session that never claimed a ticket inherited the singleton `state/active_ticket` on its first beat (leak). Fixed: presence resolution is now per-session marker, else the entry's previous ticket, else null; presence never reads the singleton (which remains for the Git hook and digest). The subagent half stands by design: a subagent tree shares the parent session's single entry, one session one ring, and the entry follows the most recent `set_active_ticket`.
2. **Torn presence reads.** Reproduced: 3,553 of 26,400 reads saw an empty entry file, and `started_at` reset once mid-run (the elapsed-timer jump). Fixed with atomic temp-file-plus-rename writes; post-fix harness: 0 empty, 0 torn, one `started_at` across ~45,000 reads.
3. **Lost update on concurrent `updateTicket`.** Reproduced: 1 of 30 trials had a successful status move silently reverted by a concurrent title update from another process. Fixed with a per-project mutation lock (`state/mutate.lock`, mkdir-as-mutex with owner token, atomic rename steal after 30 s, 5 s timeout) around every load-modify-write-reindex span, `setColumnOrder` included. Post-fix: 0 lost in 30 trials, and the regression test asserts every successful move is durable.
4. **Ticket missing from the index during concurrent reindex.** Reproduced: in 247,800 polls, 885 caught index.json mid-write and 35 caught a well-formed index missing a ticket entirely. Fixed by the atomic writes (tickets, comments, sessions, host documents, index.json, BOARD.md, board order, graph layout). Post-fix: 0 torn, 0 missing in 252,100 polls.
5. **App drops change events during its own mutations.** Confirmed from `apps/desktop/src/state/store.tsx` (events returned, never replayed, during the mutation plus a 500 ms tail). Fixed with a change gate (`change-gate.ts`): in-window events are collapsed and replayed as one reload when the outermost mutation window closes; the replay never raises the external-change toast, since an in-window event is almost always the app's own write echoing back.

## Regression coverage

- `packages/core/test/concurrency.spec.ts` (15 tests): multi-process stress for presence atomicity, index integrity and lost updates (child workers under `test/workers/` import the built dist and fail loudly when it is missing); lock wait, atomic steal, stolen-mid-mutation release, timeout; presence attribution; `writeFileAtomic` content and mode preservation.
- `apps/desktop/test/changeGate.spec.ts` (6) and `store.spec.tsx` (1): deferral, single replay, overlapping windows, no toast on replay.
- Inverted the two tests that asserted the old singleton-fallback behaviour (`packages/core/test/settings.spec.ts`, `packages/mcp/test/helper.spec.ts`).

## Accepted residuals

- A mutation legitimately holding the lock beyond 30 s (machine sleep mid-write) can be stolen; the owner token stops the release cascade but two writers could overlap in that window.
- A crash between temp write and rename leaves a dot-prefixed `.tmp` file; invisible to every reader, nothing sweeps it.
- Windows `renameSync` can fail transiently with `EPERM` under antivirus holds; no retry yet.
- A cleared active ticket keeps showing on the ring until the next turn's presence-start (the beat preserves the entry's previous ticket).

## Acceptance criteria

- Each mechanism either reproduced and fixed, or recorded here as not reproducible with the attempt described. (All five confirmed and fixed.)
- Regression coverage for whatever is fixed. (Above.)
- Round-trip fidelity and index determinism are unaffected. (Existing suites pass unchanged.)
- `pnpm test` and `pnpm build` pass. (Both green across the workspace.)

## Non-goals

- No new MCP tools, no queues, retries or scheduling (Lovelace is an orchestrator, not a CI system).
- No redesign of the presence model beyond what the confirmed mechanisms require (ADR-0012 stands; ADR-0016 records the concurrency mechanisms).
