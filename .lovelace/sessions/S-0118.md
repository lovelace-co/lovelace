## Approach

Continued from S-0108: drive the pipeline to its first green tag run, fixing what only real runners could reveal, with cheap branch-only diagnostic runs on 1x-billed Linux instead of full release builds.

## What happened

Release run 29213229096 on the re-pushed v0.1.0 tag went green on all five jobs and produced the draft GitHub Release: installers for every platform (two DMGs, NSIS exe, MSI, AppImage, deb, rpm), four signed updater packages, and a mechanically generated latest.json verified to carry version 0.1.0, all four platform keys sorted, correct URLs and valid signature envelopes. The journey surfaced one deep Linux bug: linuxdeploy rpath-patches every ELF in the AppDir using a patchelf embedded inside its own AppImage, and both tauri's pinned copy and upstream's (0.15) corrupt Bun compiled sidecars, proven by probe runs that patched a real sidecar and executed it (segfault with old patchelf, healthy with 0.18). The fix (ccc58b9, plus 5fc348b and the earlier libfuse2/NO_STRIP round in b2e06ed): graft patchelf 0.18 into the pinned linuxdeploy, repack it as a genuine AppImage with appimagetool, prime tauri's tool cache before the build, and execute a sidecar from inside the assembled AppDir as a permanent integrity gate in the workflow. macOS jobs warn about absent Apple signing secrets and produce unsigned installers as designed.

## Open questions

- Apple and Windows code-signing certificates remain to be procured before public launch; installers are unsigned until then, by design.
- The appimagetool continuous release is the one unpinned download in the priming step; if it ever breaks, pin a release tag.
- tauri-apps/binary-releases may some day update their pinned linuxdeploy with a fixed patchelf, making the priming step removable; tauri issue 8604 tracks the update request.
